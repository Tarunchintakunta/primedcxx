#### Dynamic Leverage

At IC, leverage is applied through a tiered margin system that automatically adjusts based on your position size. This means your trade is split across tiers, with each portion margined at the rate of the tier it falls into. As your exposure increases, margin requirements scale accordingly, resulting in progressively lower leverage on larger positions. The entire position is not assigned a single leverage rate. Instead, each part is calculated separately.

Designed to deliver competitive trading conditions across all account sizes and asset classes, the tiered margin system works seamlessly in the background, empowering traders to focus on strategy and adapt to market dynamics.

###### Example

- Instrument: USDCHF
- Position size: 70 lots
- Contract size: 1 lot = 100,000 USD
- Total position size: 70 x 100,000 = 7,000,000 USD

###### Normal market conditions

- Tier 1: 0–25 lots → 1:5000
- Tier 2: 25–50 lots → 1:3000
- Tier 3: 50+ lots -> 1:1000

###### How the margin is calculated

- Tier 1 Calculation (0 – 25 lots) 25 × 100,000 = 2,500,000 USD Margin required = 2,500,000 ÷ 5000 = 500 USD
- Tier 2 Calculation (25.00 – 50 lots) 25.00 × 100,000 = 2,500,000 USD Margin required = 2,500,000 ÷ 3000 = 833.33 USD
- Tier 3 Calculation (50.00+ lots) 20 × 100,000 = 2,000,000 USD USD Margin required = 2,000,000 ÷ 1000 = 2,000.00 USD

###### Total margin required

- 500 USD + 833.33 USD + 2,000 USD = 3,333.33 USD

Leverage may change and impact your margin requirements. Please ensure sufficient funds are maintained.

#### Higher Margin Requirement (HMR)

Some trading periods are subject to Higher Margin Requirements (HMR), during which available leverage is temporarily capped to help manage risk during elevated market volatility. This may apply around end-of-day roll, major news events, and weekends. HMR does not change how margin is calculated. The same incremental tiered margin logic continues to apply, meaning each leverage tier is applied only to the portion of your position that falls within that tier's lot range, not to the full position.

- End of day : Daily 30 minutes before market close and 15 minutes after the market opens.
- Weekend : Every Friday, 1 hour before market close until Monday, 30 minutes after the market opens on Monday.
- Major News : 15 minutes before until 1 minute after a high-impact news release.

###### Example

- Instrument: USDCHF
- Position size: 70 lots
- Contract size: 1 lot = 100,000 USD
- Total position size: 70 x 100,000 = 7,000,000 USD

###### Tiers During HMR

- Tier 1: 0–25 lots → 1:500
- Tier 2: 25–50 lots → 1:200
- Tier 3: 50+ lots → 1:200

###### How Margin is calculated during HMR periods

- Tier 1 Calculation (0 – 25 lots) 25 × 100,000 = 2,500,000 USD Margin required = 2,500,000 ÷ 500 = 5,000.00 USD
- Tier 2 Calculation (25.00 – 50 lots) 25.00 × 100,000 = 2,500,000 USD Margin required = 2,500,000 ÷ 200 = 12,500 USD
- Tier 3 Calculation (50.00+ lots) 20 × 100,000 = 2,000,000 USD Margin required = 2,000,000 ÷ 200 = 10,000.00 USD

###### Total margin required during HMR

- 5,000 USD + 12,500 USD + 10,000.00 USD = 27,500.00 USD

These rates apply to new positions only. Existing open positions retain the leverage at which they were opened. When the HMR period ends, your margin is automatically recalculated based on the prevailing mark-to-market value at that time, which may result in higher or lower margin compared to when the positions were originally opened.

### FAQs

##### How is margin calculated with tiered leverage?

Margin is calculated progressively by tier. Your position is split into portions, and each portion is margined at the leverage of the tier it falls into, rather than applying a single leverage rate to the entire trade.

##### Why does leverage decrease as my position size increases?

As your exposure grows, portions of your position move into higher tiers where margin requirements are higher. This helps manage risk while still offering higher leverage on smaller portions of your trade.

##### What are Higher Margin Requirement (HMR) periods?

HMR periods are times of increased market risk, such as major news events, (Central Bank Rate Decisions, Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP), CPI , Core CPI , Crude Oil Inventories) market close, or weekends, when leverage is temporarily reduced and margin requirements are increased for new positions. The events listed are indicative and not exhaustive. The Company reserves the right to implement HMR conditions at any time, at its sole discretion, where deemed necessary due to market volatility or prevailing market conditions.

##### What happens to my margin after an HMR period ends?

Once the HMR period ends, margin is automatically recalculated based on the current market value of your positions, and standard leverage conditions resume for new trades.

##### Does HMR affect my existing open positions?

In most cases, no. HMR applies only to new positions, and existing trades retain the leverage at which they were opened.
However, for certain instruments such as stocks, margin requirements on existing positions may be recalculated during HMR periods, depending on market conditions.
